Defining the role of neuronal HIF signaling in manganese-induced neurotoxicity
Manganese (Mn) is an essential metal that becomes neurotoxic when elevated, causing motor dysfunction and Parkinsonian-like neurological symptoms. Although hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF) signaling is a major regulator of cellular responses to metabolic stress and metal homeostasis, its contribution to Mn-induced neurotoxicity and neuromotor disease remains poorly understood.
As a PhD candidate in Dr. Somshuvra Mukhopadhyay’s laboratory, I am investigating how neuronal HIF signaling regulates the brain’s response to elevated Mn exposure. To define the cell-specific contributions of HIF signaling, I generated conditional knockout mouse strains targeting major HIF isoforms in dopaminergic, glutamatergic, and GABAergic neurons. Using neuromotor behavioral assays and targeted gene expression analysis of isolated neuronal populations, I have found that neuronal HIF1α and HIF2α contribute to basal motor function and am investigating their roles in Mn-induced motor dysfunction.
This work will establish a mechanistic framework for understanding how HIF signaling influences neuronal responses to environmental metal exposure. My contributions include developing genetic mouse models, establishing experimental approaches for assessing motor function, and applying molecular analyses to define cell-specific responses to Mn and HIF disruption.
