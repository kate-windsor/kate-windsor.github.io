### Translating neuroscience research into patient-centered stroke rehabilitation

Stroke recovery depends on understanding how injury to specific brain regions affects neural circuits underlying language and how targeted interventions can support functional recovery. However, effective rehabilitation also requires translating complex neuroscience concepts into accessible information that allows patients and families to understand their condition and participate in care.

As a research assistant at Austin Speech Labs, an outpatient therapy center for stroke survivors, I contributed to clinical research and rehabilitation efforts focused on language recovery following stroke. I designed and implemented a constraint-induced speech therapy program for individuals with Broca’s aphasia and administered bilingual speech therapy to investigate how therapy language influences anomia rehabilitation. I also tracked speech assessment outcomes and developed visual summaries of patient progress to communicate treatment outcomes to families.

Through direct interaction with stroke survivors and their families, I gained experience translating neurobiological concepts into understandable and actionable information. This experience strengthened my ability to communicate complex biomedical discoveries across diverse audiences and reinforced the importance of connecting neuroscience research with patient-centered applications.
